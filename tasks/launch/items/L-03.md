## Finding confirmation

Current behavior:

- A signed-out recipient can resolve a valid invite and select a language, but continuing opens generic sign-up without the invite token, inviter, or selected language.
- The screen tells the recipient to sign in and reopen the invite manually.
- Successful email and Google authentication always route to `/chats`; neither path claims the invite.

Evidence:

- Audit: [`P0-02`](../../mvp-launch-audit-2026-07-15.md#p0-02-a-new-invite-recipient-cannot-finish-the-central-onboarding-journey)

## Decision record

Required decision: `None. The audit launch gate and invite product journey both require automatic continuation after authentication.`

Options and tradeoffs:

- Keep continuation only in the auth route. This survives switching between sign-up and login and native Google authentication without storing sensitive or stale state on disk.
- Persist continuation locally. This could survive process death during auth, but adds lifecycle, expiry, account-switching, and cleanup behavior beyond the confirmed failure.

Approved decision: `Carry the complete continuation in the auth route and consume it after successful authentication. Keep invite status authoritative on the server.`

Approved by/date: `Derived from P0-02 and US-024 through US-026, 2026-07-16`

## Scope

In scope:

- Carry the invite token, inviter display name, and selected learning language into sign-up/sign-in using encoded route parameters.
- Preserve that continuation while switching between sign-up and login on the auth screen.
- After successful email or Google authentication, atomically claim the invite, refresh chats, and open the resulting chat.
- Route expired, already-claimed, and missing invite outcomes back through the token resolver for an authoritative status screen.
- Keep recoverable claim errors on auth with concise user-facing feedback and a retry path.

Out of scope:

- Android App Link verification and release signing; tracked by L-04.
- Duplicate pair-chat prevention beyond the existing atomic invite claim; tracked by L-11.
- Push notifications promised by US-025; tracked by L-17.
- Durable invite continuation after force-stop/process death during authentication.
- Changes to invite expiry, single-use semantics, schema, or RLS.

Files/systems expected to change:

- `lib/features/invite/invite_pick_language_screen.dart`
- `lib/features/invite/invite_landing_screen.dart`
- `lib/features/invite/invite_continuation.dart` or equivalent focused state helper
- `lib/features/auth/auth_screen.dart`
- `lib/app/router.dart`
- Focused invite/auth tests
- This work record and `tasks/launch/backlog.md`

Migration, rollout, or rollback concerns:

- No database migration is required; `claim_invite` remains the server-side authority.
- Existing auth URLs without invite parameters must retain normal `/chats` behavior.
- Rollback restores the broken reopen-the-link journey and is not acceptable for launch.

## Implementation record

Summary:

- Invite state now has one encoded continuation model containing the token, inviter display name, and selected learning language.
- The language picker passes that continuation into auth instead of asking signed-out users to reopen the link.
- Email sign-up, email login, and Google auth all consume the same post-auth continuation and open the claimed chat.
- Successful auth is retained across transient claim failures so retrying does not repeat account creation or login.
- Known terminal server failures return through `/i/<token>` so the resolver renders authoritative expired, used, missing, or owner state.
- Signed-in direct claims and post-auth claims share one provider that invokes `claim_invite` and refreshes the chat list.
- The local integration helper now runs real auth and invite RPCs with independent clients, including a concurrent Bob/Carol claim and service-role-controlled expiry.
- Integration artifacts are deleted in `finally`; the service-role key is read at runtime from the local CLI and is never stored in Git.
